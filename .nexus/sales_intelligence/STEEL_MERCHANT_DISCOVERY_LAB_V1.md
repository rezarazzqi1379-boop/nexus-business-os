# NEXUS Steel Merchant Discovery Lab v1 — Esfarayen/EICO adjacency

## Objective
Find merchants, stockists, importers/exporters, processors and end users that can buy/sell products adjacent to Esfarayen Industrial Complex capabilities without confusing capability, catalogue, shipment or customer-reference evidence with a verified transaction.

## New discovery methods
1. Shipment Reverse: start from HS 7228 / forged-alloy descriptions and pivot exporter → foreign buyer → buyer's other suppliers.
2. Procurement Reverse: grade/form → buyer tender → repeated buyer → award/winner → historical supplier graph.
3. Application Reverse: shafts, gears, rolls, drilling tools, thick-wall components, mining crusher shafts, oil/gas/petrochemical parts → manufacturers/MRO/EPC → raw-material demand.
4. Supplier-Customer Reverse: public customer/reference pages of steel-service suppliers → candidate steel consumers; verify independently.
5. Trader Graph: catalogue grade + stock dimensions + represented mills/origins + warehouse/service capability → trader/stockist → downstream buyer discovery.
6. Failed-Tender Mining: no-bid/failed alloy-steel tenders become scarcity hypotheses and future-RFQ watch targets, never scarcity facts.
7. Machine-Shop/Forge Adjacency: heavy machining/forging firms are potential intermediate buyers even when end user is elsewhere.
8. Certificate/QA Reverse: ISO/IATF/API/project certificates and material-testing capabilities identify sectors and likely material families.
9. People-to-Company Reverse: procurement/materials/supply/technical roles reveal company demand questions; role does not prove authority.
10. Competitor-Customer Shadowing: competitor/reference/customer lists generate candidates; each relationship must be rebound to independent evidence.
11. Import-Substitution Gap: compare imported alloy/forged products against evidenced EICO capability; similarity is not equivalence.
12. Export Mirror: start from Iranian HS7228 exporters and discover foreign consignee/buyer markets, then identify lookalike buyers by application.
13. Maintenance Demand: steel mills, mines, cement, power, petrochemical and large rotating-equipment operators consume replacement shafts/rolls/gears/forgings.
14. Project Trigger: expansions, overhauls, shutdowns, new production lines and localization programs create time-bounded demand signals.
15. Material Family Graph: Russian/DIN/EN/ASTM grade names are separate nodes connected only by evidenced standard/equivalence, never text similarity.

## Current public signals
- Public trade-data search exposes a large Iranian buyer universe for steel/forgings and a narrower round/alloy-steel universe; use as discovery leads, not verified buyer truth.
- Public HS7228 supplier data identifies EICO as an observed Iranian exporter, supporting export-buyer reverse discovery.
- Public EICO descriptions show forged alloy-steel applications across mining, oil/gas/petrochemical, automotive, machinery, shipbuilding and power sectors.
- A public industry interview names MAPNA and Mobarakeh as historical EICO customers; historical reference only, not current purchasing state.
- Azin Forge in Esfarayen is a nearby 40,000 t/y forged-parts manufacturer and a high-value adjacency node for raw-material/processing ecosystem research.
- Public supplier/customer pages can reveal companies that sell into EICO and other Iranian mills; treat these as candidate relationship edges until independently verified.
